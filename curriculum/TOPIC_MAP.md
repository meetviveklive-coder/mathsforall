# Topic map — for review

58 topics, about 25 hours of learning time. Numbers in brackets are prerequisites. Generated from `curriculum/topic-map.json` — edit that file, not this one.

## Stage 0 — Foundations

### Numbers

**1. Counting and numbers to 20** · [start] · 15 min

- Count objects up to 20 and say how many
- Read and write numerals 0 to 20
- Explain that zero means none
- *Watch for:* Counting an object twice or skipping one; Thinking the last number said is just a label, not the total

**2. Numbers to 100: tens and ones** · [1] · 20 min

- Group objects into tens and ones
- Read and write two-digit numbers
- Say the value of each digit in a two-digit number
- *Watch for:* Writing 'forty-six' as 406; Thinking the 4 in 46 means four, not forty

**3. Comparing and ordering numbers** · [2] · 20 min

- Use <, > and = correctly
- Place numbers on a number line
- Arrange a set of numbers in ascending and descending order
- *Watch for:* Comparing by the last digit instead of the first; Reading the < sign backwards

**4. Place value to thousands** · [2] · 25 min

- Read and write numbers up to 9,999
- Write a number in expanded form (3,405 = 3000 + 400 + 5)
- Explain why zero is needed as a placeholder
- *Watch for:* Dropping the zero in 3,405 and writing 345; Thinking a digit has the same value in every position

**5. Large numbers: lakh and crore** · [4] · 25 min

- Read and write numbers up to 99,99,99,999 using the Indian system
- Place commas correctly in the Indian system
- Write a large number in words
- *Watch for:* Using groups of three digits throughout (1,000,000 instead of 10,00,000); Thinking one lakh is 1,000,000
- *Context:* Population of cities, prices of houses, cricket stadium capacities.

**6. Million and billion: two ways to write big numbers** · [5] · 25 min

- Read and write numbers using million and billion
- Convert between lakh/crore and million/billion
- Place commas correctly in the international system
- *Watch for:* Thinking 1 crore equals 1 million; Mixing the two comma systems in one number

**7. Rounding and estimation** · [4, 3] · 20 min

- Round numbers to the nearest 10, 100 and 1,000
- Estimate a sum or difference by rounding first
- Judge whether an answer is reasonable
- *Watch for:* Rounding 45 down to 40; Changing every digit after the rounding place to 9 instead of 0

### Addition and subtraction

**8. What addition means** · [1] · 20 min

- Model addition as putting groups together
- Recall addition facts up to 20
- Explain that order does not change a sum
- *Watch for:* Counting the starting number again when counting on; Thinking 3 + 5 and 5 + 3 give different answers

**9. What subtraction means** · [8] · 20 min

- Model subtraction as taking away and as finding a difference
- Use addition facts to check subtraction
- Explain why order matters in subtraction
- *Watch for:* Thinking 3 − 5 is the same as 5 − 3; Believing subtraction always means 'take away' only

**10. Adding and subtracting with regrouping** · [2, 9] · 30 min

- Add two-digit numbers with carrying
- Subtract two-digit numbers with borrowing
- Explain carrying as trading 10 ones for 1 ten
- *Watch for:* Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column (52 − 27 = 35); Forgetting to add the carried ten

**11. Adding and subtracting large numbers** · [10, 4] · 30 min

- Add and subtract numbers up to 6 digits in columns
- Borrow across zeros (5,000 − 1,248)
- Check a subtraction by adding
- *Watch for:* Misaligning digits when numbers have different lengths; Borrowing across zeros incorrectly

### Multiplication and division

**12. Multiplication as equal groups** · [8, 2] · 20 min

- Model multiplication as equal groups and as arrays
- Write repeated addition as multiplication
- Explain why 3 × 4 = 4 × 3 using an array
- *Watch for:* Thinking multiplication always makes a number bigger; Adding instead of multiplying (3 × 4 = 7)

**13. Times tables to 10 × 10** · [12] · 30 min

- Recall multiplication facts up to 10 × 10
- Use known facts to work out unknown ones (7 × 8 from 7 × 4)
- Multiply by 0 and 1 correctly
- *Watch for:* Thinking any number times 0 is that number; Memorising tables without seeing patterns

**14. Division as sharing and grouping** · [13] · 25 min

- Model division as fair sharing and as grouping
- Use multiplication facts to divide
- Find and interpret a remainder
- *Watch for:* Thinking 6 ÷ 2 equals 2 ÷ 6; Leaving a remainder larger than the divisor

**15. Multiplying larger numbers** · [13, 11] · 35 min

- Multiply by 10, 100 and 1,000
- Multiply a multi-digit number by a one-digit number
- Multiply two two-digit numbers using the column method
- *Watch for:* Forgetting the placeholder zero in the second row; Thinking 'add a zero' works for decimals too

**16. Long division** · [14, 15] · 35 min

- Divide a multi-digit number by a one-digit number
- Divide by a two-digit number
- Check division using multiplication and the remainder
- *Watch for:* Skipping a zero in the quotient (1,224 ÷ 12 = 12); Bringing down two digits at once

**17. Choosing the operation: word problems** · [16] · 30 min

- Decide which operation a word problem needs
- Solve two-step word problems
- Check that an answer makes sense in the story
- *Watch for:* Choosing an operation from keywords alone ('more' always means add); Ignoring what the remainder means in the story
- *Context:* Train tickets, sharing laddoos, market shopping, school trip budgets.

**18. Factors and multiples** · [14] · 25 min

- List all factors of a number up to 100
- List the first multiples of a number
- Find common factors and common multiples by listing
- *Watch for:* Confusing factors with multiples; Forgetting 1 and the number itself as factors

### Fractions

**19. Fractions as equal parts** · [14] · 20 min

- Name a fraction from a shape divided into equal parts
- Explain what the numerator and denominator mean
- Find a fraction of a small quantity (1/3 of 12)
- *Watch for:* Accepting unequal parts as 'quarters'; Thinking a bigger denominator means a bigger fraction
- *Context:* Sharing a roti or a pizza, slices of a watermelon.

**20. Fractions on a number line** · [19, 3] · 20 min

- Place fractions between 0 and 1 on a number line
- Place fractions greater than 1 on a number line
- Explain that a fraction is a number, not just a shape
- *Watch for:* Counting tick marks instead of spaces; Thinking fractions can only be less than 1

**21. Equivalent fractions** · [20, 18] · 25 min

- Show two fractions are equal using diagrams and the number line
- Make equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing top and bottom
- Write a fraction in its simplest form
- *Watch for:* Adding the same number to top and bottom (1/2 = 2/3); Thinking 2/4 is bigger than 1/2 because the numbers are bigger

**22. Comparing fractions** · [21] · 25 min

- Compare fractions with the same denominator or the same numerator
- Compare any two fractions using a common denominator
- Order a set of fractions
- *Watch for:* Thinking 1/8 > 1/4 because 8 > 4; Comparing only numerators when denominators differ

**23. Improper fractions and mixed numbers** · [20] · 20 min

- Convert an improper fraction to a mixed number
- Convert a mixed number to an improper fraction
- Place mixed numbers on a number line
- *Watch for:* Writing 2 1/3 as 3/3; Thinking an improper fraction is 'wrong'

**24. Adding and subtracting fractions with the same denominator** · [23, 21] · 20 min

- Add and subtract fractions with the same denominator
- Explain why the denominator stays the same
- Give answers greater than 1 as mixed numbers
- *Watch for:* Adding denominators too (1/5 + 2/5 = 3/10)

**25. Adding and subtracting fractions with different denominators** · [24] · 30 min

- Find a common denominator
- Add and subtract fractions with different denominators
- Add and subtract mixed numbers
- *Watch for:* Adding tops and bottoms separately (1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5); Changing the denominator without changing the numerator

**26. Multiplying fractions** · [21, 23] · 30 min

- Find a fraction of a whole number
- Multiply two fractions and explain it with an area model
- Multiply mixed numbers
- *Watch for:* Thinking multiplying always makes a number bigger; Multiplying the whole parts and fraction parts of mixed numbers separately

**27. Dividing fractions** · [26] · 30 min

- Explain division by a fraction as 'how many fit into'
- Divide using the reciprocal
- Solve sharing problems involving fractions
- *Watch for:* Flipping the first fraction instead of the second; Thinking dividing always makes a number smaller

### Decimals

**28. Decimals: tenths and hundredths** · [20, 4] · 25 min

- Read and write tenths, hundredths and thousandths
- Connect decimals to fractions with denominators 10, 100, 1000
- Use decimals for rupees and paise, metres and centimetres
- *Watch for:* Thinking 0.25 is bigger than 0.3 because 25 > 3; Reading 0.05 as 'zero point five'
- *Context:* ₹12.50 is 12 rupees and 50 paise.

**29. Comparing and rounding decimals** · [28, 7] · 20 min

- Compare and order decimals
- Place decimals on a number line
- Round decimals to a given number of places
- *Watch for:* Thinking longer decimals are always bigger; Ignoring the place value of the digits after the point

**30. Adding and subtracting decimals** · [28, 11] · 25 min

- Line up decimal points to add and subtract
- Use zeros to fill empty places
- Solve money problems with rupees and paise
- *Watch for:* Lining up the last digits instead of the decimal points

**31. Multiplying and dividing decimals** · [30, 16, 26] · 35 min

- Multiply and divide decimals by 10, 100 and 1,000
- Multiply a decimal by a decimal
- Divide a decimal by a whole number and by a decimal
- *Watch for:* Keeping the decimal point in the same place after multiplying by 10; Placing the decimal point by counting from the left

**32. Converting between fractions and decimals** · [21, 28, 16] · 25 min

- Convert a decimal to a fraction in simplest form
- Convert a fraction to a decimal by division
- Recognise recurring decimals such as 1/3 = 0.333…
- *Watch for:* Writing 1/4 as 1.4; Thinking 0.333… is exactly equal to 0.33

### Percent and ratio

**33. Percent means 'out of 100'** · [32] · 20 min

- Explain percent as a fraction out of 100
- Convert between percents, fractions and decimals
- Recognise common benchmarks (50%, 25%, 10%, 1%)
- *Watch for:* Thinking a percent cannot be more than 100; Writing 5% as 0.5

**34. Finding a percentage of an amount** · [33, 31] · 25 min

- Find a percentage of an amount using 10% and 1% steps
- Find a percentage of an amount by multiplication
- Express one quantity as a percentage of another
- *Watch for:* Finding 20% by dividing by 20; Using the wrong whole when expressing as a percentage

**35. Discounts, GST and percentage change** · [34] · 30 min

- Calculate a price after a discount
- Calculate a price after adding GST
- Find the percentage increase or decrease between two values
- *Watch for:* Thinking a 20% rise then a 20% fall returns to the start; Dividing by the new value instead of the original
- *Context:* Festival-sale discounts, GST on a bill, rise in petrol prices.

**36. Ratio** · [21] · 25 min

- Write a ratio to compare two quantities
- Simplify a ratio
- Share an amount in a given ratio
- *Watch for:* Treating 2:3 as 2/3 of the whole instead of 2 parts out of 5; Writing a ratio in the wrong order
- *Context:* Mixing rice and dal, sharing prize money, recipe ingredients.

**37. Proportion and the unitary method** · [36, 31] · 30 min

- Use the unitary method to find the cost of many from the cost of one
- Decide whether two quantities are in proportion
- Solve rate problems (speed, price per kg)
- *Watch for:* Using addition instead of multiplication to scale up; Assuming every relationship is proportional

## Stage 1 — Pre-algebra

### Number theory

**38. Divisibility rules** · [18, 16] · 25 min

- Test divisibility by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9 and 10
- Explain why the rule for 3 works
- Use divisibility rules to find factors quickly
- *Watch for:* Using the digit-sum rule for 4; Thinking a number divisible by 2 and 4 must be divisible by 8

**39. Prime and composite numbers** · [38] · 25 min

- Define prime and composite numbers
- Find primes up to 100 with the Sieve of Eratosthenes
- Explain why 1 is neither prime nor composite
- *Watch for:* Thinking all odd numbers are prime; Thinking 1 is prime

**40. Prime factorisation** · [39] · 25 min

- Write a number as a product of primes using a factor tree
- Use repeated division to factorise
- Explain why every number has exactly one prime factorisation
- *Watch for:* Stopping at a composite factor; Thinking different factor trees give different answers

**41. HCF and LCM** · [40] · 35 min

- Find the HCF of two or three numbers
- Find the LCM of two or three numbers
- Choose HCF or LCM to solve a word problem
- Use HCF × LCM = product of two numbers
- *Watch for:* Confusing HCF and LCM; Thinking the LCM is always the product of the numbers
- *Context:* Bells ringing together at a temple, cutting cloth into equal pieces.

### Integers

**42. Negative numbers and integers** · [3, 9] · 20 min

- Place negative numbers on a number line
- Compare and order integers
- Use integers for temperature, height above sea level, and money owed
- *Watch for:* Thinking −8 is greater than −3; Thinking negative numbers are not real numbers
- *Context:* Winter temperatures in Leh, depth below sea level, a shopkeeper's credit.

**43. Adding and subtracting integers** · [42, 11] · 30 min

- Add integers using a number line
- Subtract an integer by adding its opposite
- Solve problems involving changes in temperature or money
- *Watch for:* Thinking subtracting a negative makes the number smaller; Applying 'two negatives make a positive' to addition

**44. Multiplying and dividing integers** · [43, 16] · 25 min

- Multiply and divide integers with the correct sign
- Explain why a negative times a negative is positive using patterns
- Evaluate products with several negative factors
- *Watch for:* Thinking (−3) × (−4) = −12; Getting the sign right but forgetting it in the final answer

### Exponents and order

**45. Powers and exponents** · [15] · 20 min

- Write repeated multiplication using exponents
- Evaluate powers with whole-number exponents
- Explain why any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1
- *Watch for:* Thinking 3² = 6; Thinking 5⁰ = 0

**46. Squares and square roots** · [45, 40] · 25 min

- Recognise perfect squares up to 400
- Find square roots of perfect squares using prime factorisation
- Estimate the square root of a non-square number between two whole numbers
- *Watch for:* Thinking the square root of 16 is 8; Thinking square roots of non-squares do not exist

**47. Order of operations (BODMAS)** · [45, 44] · 25 min

- Evaluate expressions using the correct order of operations
- Explain that division and multiplication (and addition and subtraction) go left to right
- Use brackets to change the order
- *Watch for:* Always doing division before multiplication because D comes before M; Working strictly left to right for everything

**48. Laws of exponents** · [47, 26] · 30 min

- Multiply and divide powers with the same base
- Raise a power to a power
- Evaluate negative exponents as reciprocals
- *Watch for:* Multiplying exponents when multiplying powers (2³ × 2⁴ = 2¹²); Thinking 2⁻³ is negative

**49. Very large and very small numbers** · [48, 6, 31] · 25 min

- Write numbers in standard form (scientific notation)
- Convert from standard form to ordinary numbers
- Compare numbers written in standard form
- *Watch for:* Writing 45 × 10³ as standard form; Moving the decimal point the wrong way for small numbers
- *Context:* Distance to the Moon for Chandrayaan, India's population, size of a bacterium.

### Rational numbers

**50. Rational numbers** · [44, 27, 22, 25] · 35 min

- Define a rational number as p/q with q ≠ 0
- Place negative fractions on a number line
- Add, subtract, multiply and divide rational numbers
- *Watch for:* Thinking −1/2 and 1/−2 are different numbers; Thinking integers are not rational numbers

### Towards algebra

**51. Letters for numbers: variables and expressions** · [47] · 25 min

- Use a letter to stand for an unknown or varying number
- Write an expression from a word description
- Understand 3n as 3 × n
- *Watch for:* Thinking a letter stands for an object ('a' means apples); Thinking 3n means thirty-something

**52. Evaluating expressions** · [51, 50] · 25 min

- Substitute values into an expression
- Evaluate expressions with negative and fractional values
- Use a formula (perimeter, cost) by substitution
- *Watch for:* Substituting −3 into x² as −9; Writing 2x with x = 5 as 25

**53. Simplifying expressions: like terms and brackets** · [51] · 30 min

- Collect like terms
- Expand brackets using the distributive property
- Simplify expressions that need both
- *Watch for:* Combining unlike terms (2x + 3y = 5xy); Multiplying only the first term inside the bracket

**54. Solving one-step equations** · [52] · 25 min

- Explain an equation as a balance
- Solve equations of the form x + a = b and ax = b
- Check a solution by substituting it back
- *Watch for:* Doing an operation to one side only; Thinking x always has to be a whole number

**55. Solving two-step equations** · [54, 53] · 35 min

- Solve equations of the form ax + b = c
- Solve equations with the variable on both sides
- Solve equations that need brackets expanded first
- *Watch for:* Undoing operations in the wrong order; Changing the sign of a term without moving it across

**56. Writing and solving equations from stories** · [55, 37] · 35 min

- Define a variable for the unknown in a story
- Write an equation from a word problem
- Solve it and interpret the answer in the story
- *Watch for:* Writing the equation in the order the words appear; Forgetting to answer the question that was asked

**57. Number patterns and rules** · [51] · 25 min

- Continue a number or shape pattern
- Find the rule for the nth term of a linear pattern
- Use the rule to find any term
- *Watch for:* Only finding the term-to-term rule (add 3) and not the position rule (3n + 1)

**58. Simple interest** · [34, 52] · 25 min

- Explain principal, rate, time and interest
- Calculate simple interest using I = PRT/100
- Find the total amount repaid or received
- *Watch for:* Forgetting to convert months into years; Adding interest to the rate instead of the amount
- *Context:* A fixed deposit at a bank or post office, a loan for a bicycle.
